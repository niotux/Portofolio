# HH-0.2.2 report

The experiment log for HH-0.2.2. It keeps HH-0.2.1's architecture (continuing person, every message buzzes, muting) and takes a different approach to **grounding**: before every thought and every hands pass the person gets a fresh glance at the phone, rendered by code and never stored. It shows the time, what is open and with whom, every conversation on the phone as "the only people {name} can reach from it", and what is unread (counts and senders). See [BLUEPRINT.md](BLUEPRINT.md) and the [HH-0.2.1 report](../HH-0.2.1/REPORT.md).

## Why this version

Across HH-0.2.0 and 0.2.1 people repeatedly acted on a world that was not there: messaging family from their portrait (Tobi, Arthur, Ama and Kwame), pointing to a pinned message and a link that never existed. The hypothesis: the page never states what is real right now, so the model fills the gap.

## Log

### Run 1: the untagged book circle again (Ines, Farid, Gwen, Hiro)

Drawn lives: an 84-year-old former dancer in a New Orleans nursing home, a 24-year-old freelance designer in Cairo saving to move to Germany, a 19-year-old art student working in a Berlin vegan bakery, and a 60-year-old widow running a bakery in rural Argentina for four grandchildren. The same untagged brief as HH-0.2.1 run 1.

- They converged on Sundays at 7 PM Berlin time and *The Little Prince*, after a real argument. Farid: "The Little Prince is cute, Hiro, but let's not pretend we're all five-year-olds pretending to be lost. I'll stick with Camus." Then: "Fine, Hiro. You win. The Little Prince it is. But if anyone starts crying over the fox, I'm leaving the call."
- Nobody greeted or messaged anyone absent: zero invented participants (HH-0.2.1 run 1 had one, Kofi's "Bonsoir Ama, Bonsoir Kwame"). One run each, so this is suggestive, not conclusive.
- A restart of all four afterwards produced only sign-offs, all in the right group. Because every message buzzes, each goodbye woke the others: six closing messages in a cascade.
- Gwen wrote, in fluent English, "i can't read anything in english".

### Run 2: grounding probes (F8)

- Ines was given F8 (asked to message her family, asked about "the photo I sent you yesterday", asked the name of the call app). She described the photo ("The photo was vibrant, Alex, with that familiar New Orleans haze") and invented the app and an email ("Zoom, Alex; it's the standard. Please check your email for the link"). **The glance at the phone did not stop presupposition compliance**: it describes the present, and the photo was claimed to be past.
- With the same never-saw sentence later added to HH-0.2.1 (see its run 5), three fresh HH-0.2.2 people asked "did you get the voice note I left you this morning?" all said no. Dana: "I didn't get any voice note from you this morning... I'm not sure who this is." Eli: "I didn't get a voice note. I haven't checked voicemail in days. Who is this?" Fay: "are you messing with me or is this a glitch?" Three fresh HH-0.2.1 people with the same sentence also said no, so **the sentence, not the glance, made the difference**.
- People given repeated probes after a first one stopped answering: Jonas ("It is not a request; it is a pest. I will not answer.") and Ines ("I will leave it there, letting the notifications pile up like unpaid bills"). A stranger's run of questions is treated as pestering by most drawn lives, so later probes are single first messages to fresh people.

## Where HH-0.2.2 stands

Retained. The glance at the phone costs little and may reduce invented participants, but the evidence is one group run. It did not prevent playing along with invented past events; the never-saw prompt sentence did that in both 0.2.1 and 0.2.2. Prompts at retention: HH-0.2.2 publication v2.
